# Sources

EMIL's numbers come from the open literature below, from physical reasoning written next to the constants in the
code, or from tuning on the benches in `bench/`. No code or data of any other character controller is used.

## Body model (anthropometry)
- de Leva P. (1996). Adjustments to Zatsiorsky-Seluyanov's segment inertia parameters. J Biomech 29(9):1223-1230.
  Segment mass fractions, centre-of-mass positions, radii of gyration (tables as published by the BMClab notebook,
  Duarte M., "Body segment parameters", https://github.com/BMClab/BMC). Standing centre of mass at about 55-56 % of
  stature.
- Winter D.A. (2009). Biomechanics and Motor Control of Human Movement, 4th ed., Wiley. Segment lengths as fractions
  of height (ankle height, shoulder height, shoulder width).
- ANSUR II, 2012 U.S. Army Anthropometric Survey, male public working database (n = 4082),
  https://www.openlab.psu.edu/ansur2/. Trunk breadths and depths, limb girths (capsule radii), neck and head sizes.
- NHANES 1988-1994 (CDC), biacromial breadth of adult men, used for the stance width on a swaying surface.

- Head shape (`core/skull_mesh.h`): generated by our own procedural generator: a smooth union of ellipsoids and
  capsules sized from male head anthropometry (head length 19.6 cm, breadth 15.4 cm, crown to chin 24.5 cm, cheekbones
  13.6 cm, jaw angles 10.4 cm), sampled along icosphere directions; the stored mesh is the convex hull of 135 points.

## Joint ranges and strength
- Greene W.B., Heckman J.D. (1994). The Clinical Measurement of Joint Motion. American Academy of Orthopaedic
  Surgeons. Normal adult ranges of motion; cross-checked with Soucie J.M. et al. (2011), Haemophilia 17(3):500-507.
- Hip abduction and rotation strength: isometric dynamometer studies, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5963550
  and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8179216.
- Anderson D.E. et al. (2007). Maximum voluntary joint torque as a function of joint angle and angular velocity.
  J Biomech 40(14):3105-3113. Order of magnitude of the joint torque limits, scaled with mass x height.
- Hill A.V. (1938). The heat of shortening and the dynamic constants of muscle. Proc R Soc B 126:136-195; Thelen D.G.
  (2003). Adjustment of muscle mechanics model parameters. J Biomech Eng 125(1):70-77. Force-velocity relation of the
  muscle model.
- Hip capsule restraint at the end of the range: cadaveric six-degree-of-freedom study,
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4655836.
- Passive ankle dorsiflexion under body weight: PubMed 30321953.

## Balance and stepping
- Pratt J., Carff J., Drakunov S., Goswami A. (2006). Capture point: a step toward humanoid push recovery.
  IEEE-RAS Humanoids. Capture point as the step trigger and target.
- Kajita S., Kanehiro F., Kaneko K., Yokoi K., Hirukawa H. (2001). The 3D linear inverted pendulum mode. IEEE/RSJ
  IROS. Prediction of the centre of mass.
- Kajita S. et al. (2003). Biped walking pattern generation by using preview control of ZMP. ICRA. Ankle torque bound
  by the support polygon.
- Hof A.L., Gazendam M.G.J., Sinke W.E. (2005). The condition for dynamic stability. J Biomech 38(1):1-8.
  Extrapolated centre of mass.
- Yin K., Loken K., van de Panne M. (2007). SIMBICON: simple biped locomotion control. ACM TOG 26(3). Foot placement
  feedback on position and velocity; torso held by the stance hip.
- Coros S., Beaudoin P., van de Panne M. (2010). Generalized biped walking control. ACM TOG 29(4). Virtual force on
  the centre of mass through the stance-leg Jacobian transpose.
- Horak F.B., Nashner L.M. (1986). Central programming of postural movements: adaptation to altered support-surface
  configurations. J Neurophysiol 55(6):1369-1381. Hip strategy.

## Foot-floor friction
- Almosnino S., Stevenson J.M., Bardana D.D., Diaconescu E.D., Dvir Z. BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil 1:19 (tables 1
  and 2), with the earlier free-moment data tabulated there (Nigg et al. 1982; Holden and Cavanagh 1991): free moment
  of the foot in walking and running.
- Negishi T., Ogihara N. (2023). Sci Rep 13: free moment definition.
- Rotational traction of shoes on sport surfaces: PubMed 19168808; Sci Rep s41598-023-48134-0; Konstanz sport-surface
  proceedings (ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/1672). Torsional friction of the sole.

## Falls and protective reactions
- Borrelli J., Creath R., Rogers M.W. (2019). Protective arm movements are modulated with fall height. J Biomech
  99:109569. Arm reaction latency.
- Choi W.J., Robinovitch S.N. et al. (2017). Neck flexor activation and head impact velocity in backward falls.
  Clin Biomech, doi 10.1016/j.clinbiomech.2017.08.007. Chin tuck and neck co-contraction.
- Moon Y. et al. (2016). Safe landing strategies during a fall: systematic review and meta-analysis. PubMed 27592402.
  Arm bracing, tuck and roll.

## Perception and reaction times
- Woods D.L. et al. (2015). Factors influencing the latency of simple reaction time. Front Hum Neurosci 9:131.
  Visual reaction time to an approaching car.
- Brown P., Rothwell J.C., Thompson P.D., Britton T.C., Day B.L., Marsden C.D. (1991). New observations on the normal
  auditory startle reflex in man. Brain 114:1891-1902. Flinch.
- Head-unrestrained gaze shifts: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2605952 and
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6879951. Head and eye share of a gaze shift.

## Optimisation
- Hansen N. (2016). The CMA Evolution Strategy: A Tutorial. arXiv:1604.00772. Own implementation in `bench/cma.h`.

- MuJoCo 3.17.0 documentation, https://mujoco.readthedocs.io.
